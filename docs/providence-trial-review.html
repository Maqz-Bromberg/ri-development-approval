What was built

Providence is now the second town with local steps. The trial area for hearings and precedent is Fox Point and Wayland, on the East Side. I chose it from the records: of the housing cases before the City Plan Commission in 2024 to 2026, more fell here than in any other compact area, and between them they cover the major route, the minor route, a staff-decided project, a rezoning, extensions, a major change, and two court challenges by neighbours.

Cumberland's output is unchanged. Seven Cumberland projects and three in level 1 towns were run through the engine before and after, and the route, steps, contacts, findings, verdict and schedule came out identical. One deliberate difference: a level 1 town's schedule no longer shows Cumberland's documented spans under its steps, since they were shown with no town named.

How far level 4 holds

The site now shows Providence as level 3 across the city and level 4 in Fox Point and Wayland, both marked trial. That is a fair description of the effort. It is a generous description of the result, for three reasons you should weigh before anyone else sees it.

  • Level 3 without the zoning ordinance. The process is read from the regulations, which is where process lives. But height, parking, setbacks and coverage come from the 2024 Zoning Atlas, and Providence amended its ordinance in 2025 and 2026. Every Providence check carries a standing flag saying so.
  • Level 4 from thin minutes. For any item with zoning relief, the minutes say only that "a discussion ensued" and that a transcript is part of the record. The transcript is available on request, not online. So the most contested hearings, such as 269 Wickenden Street, are the least described. Most of what is documented comes from one project, 27 East River Street, whose two hearings happened to have full minutes.
  • Level 2 with holes. Two of ten contacts have no details at all, and six carry a note of what could not be confirmed.

Questions for you

Each has what I did in the trial and what I would recommend. They are also on the dev page under Providence trial.

1. Is a level for part of a town the right model?

Offices and regulations are citywide, but hearings and precedent are local, so I gave Providence level 3 with a level 4 part. The part is a rough box around the projects, not the City's neighbourhood boundary. A site outside the box still sees the Fox Point and Wayland projects, labelled as the nearest record of how the same board decides.

Recommend: keep the part model, since no one will read a whole city's hearings at once. Replace the box with the City's own neighbourhood polygons before leaving trial.

2. Can level 3 be claimed when the zoning ordinance was not read?

I claimed it with a stated gap. The stricter reading is level 2 until the ordinance is read.

Recommend: your call. If the levels are a promise to the applicant, drop Providence to 2 and keep the steps visible as a trial.

3. Should the intake form ask town-specific questions?

Providence sorts projects of 10 or more units by commercial floor area, so the form now asks for it when the town is Providence. It also turns on gross floor area and parking spaces, which I did not add.

Recommend: yes, but decide the pattern now, because each new town will bring one or two of these.

4. What does a project under 10 units get in Providence?

The regulations start Commission review at 10 units, 10,000 sq ft or 50 parking spaces. Below that I show the building permit route with a flag saying that development plan review may apply and was not read. For the small developer this site is for, this is the common case, and it is the one the trial covers worst.

Recommend: read Article 19 of the ordinance next, before any more hearings.

5. May a date read by eye from a scanned page count as a decision?

Four events do, with a new "Read from a scan" mark. The alternative was to leave out the approvals of 269 Wickenden Street, 128 Wayland Avenue and 150 Pitman Street, which would have removed most of the precedent.

Recommend: allow it for now, add text recognition to the fetch step, and then require a second reader for anything recognition cannot match.

6. Should the site quote the ordinance through a court decision?

Three findings do (the C-2 height limit, one parking space per unit, and the small-lot parking exemption), and each pinpoint says the ordinance is quoted by the court. The court's text is from 2024.

Recommend: acceptable as a stopgap, and only with that label. Replace when the ordinance is read.

7. Where do supporters go?

At the October 2024 hearing five residents spoke for the project, and several asked for more bicycle parking. Hearing prep is built around objections and has no place for a request from a supporter, though it is something an applicant can act on.

Recommend: add a "what supporters asked for" kind before reading more Providence hearings. It did not come up in Cumberland.

8. Who asks the City?

Two requests are now concrete: permission to fetch its documents by script, and a planner to confirm the sheet. The second is already an open question in the statewide plan.

Recommend: one message covering both, from you, with the review of the 30 or so facts attached. I can draft the sheet from the data files.

9. North Providence or another unlike town next?

The plan asked whether the second town should be as unlike Cumberland as possible. Providence was, and it broke four assumptions (below). I would expect a small rural town to break different ones: no planner on staff, no published schedule.

Recommend: do the state and town split with Cumberland and Providence as the two test cases, then Exeter.

The statewide plan said this trial would "show what the overlay shape got wrong". It did.

Concerns

Trust

  • One reader, no confirmation. The quote check proves that each stored sentence is in the document. It does not prove I understood the document, attached the sentence to the right step, or left nothing out. The regulations run to 80 pages and I read the parts on land development closely and the rest not at all.
  • The city's own records disagree. I stored what each says and noted the difference. An applicant comparing their site with these will meet the same thing.

  • A timeline number that may mislead. The schedule shows 27 East River Street at 427 days from first appearance to preliminary approval. That is true and it is one very large riverfront project. With two spans on the minor route, any reader will take it as typical.
  • Survivors only. Every project read was approved. Withdrawn and denied projects exist in the agendas (14 Cargill Street was withdrawn) and none is in the trial area's data. The same gap is already an open question for Cumberland.

People

  • Residents are named in the Providence minutes. None is named on the site. I described each by role and ran a name scan over the data files, which found none. The court decision names seven neighbours as appellants. They appear as "neighbours, on appeal".
  • The minutes record one resident's repeated catchphrase. I left it out and kept the objection. A rule is worth writing down: the site repeats the substance of a concern, never the manner.
  • City staff are named as contacts, as the City's own form and page name them. Cumberland's contacts do the same. Staff change, and the read date is the only guard.
  • An individual applicant is named on the agenda for 150 Pitman Street. I did not list him as a party, since the party check cannot tell a person from a company. That check is already on the dev page as unfinished.

Tone and liability

  • The plan warned that statewide the site reads as an authority. Providence is where that bites first: it is the largest city, its staff are named, and the site now tells applicants that staff can decide a plan without a hearing. If that sentence is wrong for a given site, the cost is real. The trial mark is on the header, the footer, the coverage note, the address lookup and the route cards. I would not remove any of them.
  • The findings repeat a court's remark that a lot split to avoid parking was "troubling". It is quoted, and it names a project. Consider whether precedent notes should carry criticism of a named project at all.

Known defects left in the trial

  • State-law steps keep town wording. The building and occupancy steps shown for Providence still say "Town Council" and "Town Clerk" in places, because they are the shared text.
  • The glossary defines minor and major by the state numbers. I removed the hover from the route name for Providence. The same words inside body text still open the state definition.
  • An adjustment does not raise the hearing documents. See the table above. A Providence applicant asking only for height sees the notice and stenographer items as not applying, when a Commission meeting does apply.
  • The what-if tool and the verdict were not reviewed for Providence. They call the same engine and should follow, but I did not read their text.
  • Coastal review has no site fact. The Coastal Resources Management Council appears when "wetlands" is ticked, as a stand-in.
  • 135 Gano Street has no map pin and no known outcome.
  • The district snapshot. Providence's Atlas districts load live, with no bundled copy, so the district list is empty offline.
  • The monthly action does not look for new Providence meetings.
